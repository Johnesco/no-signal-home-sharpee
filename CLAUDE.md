## Project Context

The player is a grey-market salvager who drifted into range of the derelict after running low on fuel on a long haul. They dock, board to scavenge, and lose the tug. Twenty-six rooms across the tug and three decks of The Stillwater; four NPCs plus the ship's AI (SOMS); five designed endings, of which two exist: Escape Alone and Override The AI. Every combat encounter is optional, most puzzles have more than one solution, and deaths are always telegraphed.

## Writing Style Rules

- **Short, punchy descriptions.** No walls of text. Classic 80s Infocom feel.
- **Show, don't tell.** Depth comes through interaction, not prose.
- **PC voice:** starts sparse; memories and observations emerge through examining objects. Dry humour, dark commentary. Not chatty.
- **Corporate satire** in the world's flavour: slogans, safety posters, absurd memos.
- **Never describe what is obvious from exits.** The Chord edition follows this; the TypeScript room texts did not.
- Prose uses nautical directions (fore, aft, port, starboard). Input is compass. See deviations.

## Key Design Decisions (do not change without discussion)

- 26 rooms across 4 regions: the tug (2), Lower Deck (12), Mid Deck (7), Upper Deck (5). The regions are Chord regions; crossing into the Lower Deck flips the story to `boarded`.
- The docking sequence is four one-way states on `the docking controls` (approach, maneuvered, connected, sealed) plus two quality facts the player can examine: `the thrusters` (cold / burned) and `the pressure readout` (idle / live). Skipping the brake loses the tug; skipping the pressure check kills you in the Forward Corridor. Both are `define sequence` clocks and entity clauses, not a state machine.
- The two collision clocks are sequences: `at turn 10` while the alarm screams; `20 turns later` after it is silenced, while the controls are still `approach`.
- The seal fails three turns after boarding (`define sequence the seal gives way`), with the outcome read from the thrusters' state.
- Memories (the guidance system's first-examine observations) are `after the player examining, once` clauses on the entity.
- Story verbs (maneuvering, braking, connecting, pressure-checking, sealing, prying, overriding, launching) are `define action` blocks; the ones that act on a thing dispatch through traits (`pryable`, `overridable`, `launchable`). The cables are `cuttable with the cable snips` with an `on the player cutting` clause.
- The pressure hatch is `a door, openable` that `starts open`, gated by the cockpit's `south is blocked while the docking controls is not sealed`. Doors with keys (`cargo bulkhead`, `bridge door`, the captain's desk) start locked and are opened by the player, never by a rule.
- Override The AI requires the trunk cables severed first; the panel "politely reverses" the input otherwise.
- All player-facing text lives in the story; refusal and blocked-exit keys in the `define phrases en-US` catalog; multi-paragraph texts in `define phrase … end phrase` blocks.

### Project-specific deviations

- **Nautical directions are compass-only until #34 lands.** Chord's direction set is closed, but a 2026-09-08 spike showed `extend action going` with bare-word patterns (`fore` / `means direction north`) works at 3.6.0. The prose stays nautical; input is compass until the ticket is done. Details in `docs/chord-edition.md` §1.
- **The alarm nags instead of blocking.** Chord has no global action interceptor. A header daemon pushes the player toward the cockpit every turn while the alarm screams; the ten-turn clock still kills.
- The 3.6.0 migration and the two endings were done in one session under #32; the endings arguably deserved their own ticket.
